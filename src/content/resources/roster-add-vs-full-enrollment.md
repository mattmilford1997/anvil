Two vendors both tell you they can get your clinicians "in-network in 30 days." One is describing roster-add. The other is describing something that would be impossible. Knowing the difference is the difference between a good decision and a surprise in year two.

## Roster-add, explained
An entity (a professional corporation, a group) already holds a participation agreement with a payer. That agreement has a roster of clinicians who bill under it. Adding a clinician to the roster requires the payer to credential that individual (license, education, history) and load them under the group's existing contract and rates. Because the contract, the rates, the tax ID, and the group NPI all already exist, this is a lighter process. Some payers with delegated credentialing arrangements turn it around in days. Most turn it around in a few weeks.

What you get: your clinician billing insured visits, quickly, under someone else's agreement. What you don't get: an agreement of your own.

## Full enrollment, explained
Your entity applies to participate with the payer. The payer evaluates your entity (ownership, structure, specialties, geography, whether the panel is open), negotiates or assigns rates, executes a contract, then credentials each clinician under it. The first payer for a new entity in a new state can take four to six months. National density takes years for a company doing it alone.

What you get: contracts in your entity's name, rates you can renegotiate, relationships you can take with you. What it costs: time.

## Why "30 days" is always roster-add
No vendor can compress the payer's own contracting cycle to 30 days. Any 30-day claim is roster-add on the vendor's entity. That is fine, as long as you know it. The failure mode is thinking you bought full enrollment when you bought roster-add, and discovering the difference when an investor asks for your contract list.

## Why you need both
Roster-add is how you get to revenue this quarter. Full enrollment of your own entity is how you own the asset by next year. A company that only does the first is renting its insured revenue. A company that only does the second is unfunded by the time it works.

The sequence that works: roster-add on day one, entity formation and full enrollment starting the same week, volume migrating from the shared roster to your own contracts as each payer comes online. Nobody has to choose between speed and ownership if the two processes run in parallel.

## Questions to ask any vendor
1. Whose entity holds the payer contracts my clinicians will bill under?
2. Will you form and contract my own entity, and when does that start?
3. What is the process for moving volume from your entity to mine?
4. When I leave, which contracts come with me?

If the answers are "ours," "we don't do that," "there isn't one," and "none," you are buying roster-add only. Price it accordingly and plan the second step with someone else.
