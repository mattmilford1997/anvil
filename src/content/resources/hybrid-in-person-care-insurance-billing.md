Interventional psychiatry with in-clinic procedures. In-home care with visiting clinicians. ABA delivered in the home and the clinic. School-based therapy. Intensive outpatient programs. Each of these is a real, growing category, and each has been told by at least one billing platform "we only support virtual."

## Why telehealth platforms say no
Three assumptions in the telehealth-only model do not hold for hybrid care.

*Virtual place of service.* Telehealth billing uses telehealth place-of-service codes and modifiers under the payer's telehealth policy. In-person visits use different place-of-service codes, and many payers require the rendering location to be credentialed or enrolled as a service site. A platform that has never enrolled a physical location cannot bill a visit that happened at one.

*Leased clinicians, no facility.* The clinician-leasing model puts a clinician on a contracted entity's roster. It does not put a clinic, a home health agency, or a program on anyone's roster. Procedures, in-home services, and programs are often billed by the entity at a site, not just by the clinician.

*Visit-level claims.* Telehealth platforms model a claim as a visit with a code. Hybrid care includes drugs and supplies (with their own codes and acquisition documentation), procedures with global periods, units-based programs with authorizations, and travel or home-visit add-ons. The data model is different.

## What changes in hybrid billing
- *Site-of-service enrollment.* Each physical location may need to be enrolled with each payer, with its own address, taxonomy, and sometimes a site visit.
- *Entity in the state.* In-person care almost always requires a properly formed in-state entity and a physician owner licensed there. This is the state layer from earlier this month, and it is not optional for hybrid.
- *Procedure and drug billing.* Documentation of the procedure, the drug (NDC, dose, wastage), and medical necessity, with prior authorization for many interventional treatments.
- *Program authorizations.* IOP, PHP, and ABA are authorized by program and units, not by visit.
- *Clinician licensure and supervision by state.* In-home and school-based services often have specific supervision and credentialing requirements.

## How hybrid companies get national anyway
The pattern that works, from the interventional psychiatry, in-home care, ABA, and school-based programs billing through Anvil:

1. Own your entity in each state you deliver in-person care. There is no rented shortcut for a physical location.
2. Use a partner for the labor: entity formation, physician owner placement, site and clinician credentialing, program authorizations, and RCM built for procedures and units.
3. For any virtual component of the model, launch on a rented entity for speed while your own entity is contracted, exactly as a telehealth-only company would.
4. Run one revenue cycle across both. Separate billing operations for virtual and in-person is how hybrid companies lose track of their own economics.

## Why this matters more than it looks
Hybrid models are where a lot of the clinical and financial upside in behavioral health and chronic care lives, because they can treat patients that virtual-only cannot. A billing partner that only does virtual quietly caps your care model. Choose the partner for the care you want to deliver in three years, not the care you deliver today.
