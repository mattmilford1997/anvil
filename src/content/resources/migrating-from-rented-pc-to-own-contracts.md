Companies on a network rental model reach the same moment: a diligence question, a rate dispute, a renewal, or simply the realization that two years of insured revenue built no asset. The instinct is to switch. The better move is to migrate.

## Step 0: Do not terminate anything
The shared entity is your revenue. It stays live until the last payer in the last state is contracted with your own PC. Every failed migration we have seen started with a termination notice sent before the replacement contracts existed.

## Step 1: Inventory what you're billing today
By payer, by state, by clinician: claim volume, paid amounts, denial rates. This is the map for sequencing, and it is the evidence base for your first rate negotiations. Get the data out now, while you still have access, in a format you own.

## Step 2: Form and credential your own PC
In your top states first. Physician owner, management services agreement, entity credentialing, group NPI, clearinghouse enrollment. Weeks for formation; the contracting is the long pole.

## Step 3: Contract in order of volume
Rank payer-state pairs by insured revenue. Contract your PC with the top pairs first. Two payers in three states typically cover the majority of volume. Do not start with the easy panels; start with the ones that matter.

## Step 4: Migrate clinicians pair by pair
As each own-PC contract goes live, credential the relevant clinicians under it and route new claims for that payer in that state to your PC. The shared entity keeps billing everything else. Patients notice nothing; the clinician and the visit are the same.

## Step 5: Watch three numbers
Percent of insured revenue on your own contracts (target: majority by month 12). Denial rate on your PC versus the shared entity (should converge within 90 days). Days in AR on your PC (new payers pay slower for the first cycle; plan cash accordingly).

## Step 6: Renegotiate at first renewal
With 12 months of clean claims on your own contract and the volume data from step 1, you have leverage you never had as a fee-per-visit customer of someone else's contract. This is the payoff.

## The traps
*Terminating early.* Covered above. It is the single most expensive mistake.
*Wrong sequence.* Contracting your PC with low-volume payers first because they are easier. You end up with contracts that don't move the needle and a shared entity still carrying the load.
*Losing data.* Some platforms do not make claim-level data easy to export. Ask for it before you announce a migration, not after.
*Clinician confusion.* Each clinician is now credentialed under two entities for a period. Their schedule, their pay, and their patient list should not change. The routing is a back-office function.

| Month | Milestone |
