Most care companies learn what they will be paid after they sign the contract. This report exists so you can learn it before.

## Methodology
Data: 41,300 paid claims across 27 payers and 12 states, July through December 2026, drawn from three sources: de-identified claims billed through Anvil, de-identified claims contributed by rate lookup users, and published Medicaid fee schedules. Figures are medians of allowed amounts for the professional component, telehealth and in-person combined unless noted. We report ranges and medians, never a single payer's rate for a single group. Sample sizes under 30 claims per cell are suppressed.

## Three things the data says
*1. State matters more than payer in many cases.* The same national payer's median 90837 rate varies by up to 41% across states in our data. Founders often model "our rate with Aetna" as one number. It is a distribution.

*2. Medicaid is not uniformly the floor.* In 8 of 12 states, the Medicaid median exceeded the lowest commercial median. Behavioral health groups that write off Medicaid as low-margin should check the state.

*3. The spread is your rollout order.* If demand is similar across two states and one pays 18% more for your core codes, that is where your first contracts should go. Combine this table with the coverage map and the credentialing benchmark from November and you have a rollout plan built on three real inputs instead of a map.

## How to use it in contracting
When a payer offers rack rates, you now have the state median. A first-time contract will usually land at or below median; the point is to know where you stand so the first renewal has a target. When you own your contracts, this table is your negotiation prep.
