Most care companies we talk to want the same thing: get in network fast, then [own the contracts](/own-your-contracts). The second half of that plan has a step that is easy to miss. A payer contract is signed by a practice entity. If you do not have the right entity, the contract has nowhere to sit.

## Why the entity matters

Many states limit who can own a medical practice or employ clinicians. This is the corporate practice of medicine doctrine, usually shortened to CPOM. In those states, a company owned by non-clinicians generally cannot be the practice itself.

The common answer is the MSO-PC structure:

- A professional corporation (PC), owned by a licensed physician, delivers care, engages the clinicians and holds the payer contracts.
- A management services organization (MSO), which is your company, runs everything non-clinical: billing, technology, hiring support, marketing and operations.
- A management services agreement ties the two together, with the MSO paid a fair market value fee.

The physician who owns the PC is often called a friendly PC owner. They control clinical decisions. You run the business.

## When to start

The mistake we see most is waiting until the day you want your own contracts. By then you are months behind. Forming a PC, signing the governing documents and matching a physician owner all take time, and some states add a board or agency review that stretches things further. Payer enrollment generally cannot start until the entity exists, has a tax ID and has clinicians tied to it.

A better sequence:

1. Launch and bill on rented infrastructure so revenue starts.
2. In parallel, form your own PC and get the MSO-PC documents signed.
3. Enroll your own entity with payers while the rented setup keeps claims moving.
4. [Migrate once your contracts are live.](/solutions/switching)

Done this way, there is no gap in billing, and the contracts you end up with sit in an entity that is properly set up.

## What to get right

- The physician owner should be licensed in the states where the PC will operate.
- Clinical control has to sit with the PC. The MSO should not direct clinical care.
- Succession terms should spell out what happens if the physician owner leaves.
- The management fee should be supportable as fair market value.
- Payers will ask who owns the entity. Clean documents make credentialing smoother.
