"We're launching in Texas" is not a plan. "We're contracting with these four plans in Texas in this order" is. Here is how to get from the first sentence to the second.

## Payer concentration by state
In nearly every state, a small number of payers cover most commercial lives: typically one dominant Blue plan, one or two national carriers, and in some markets a strong regional. Medicaid follows the same pattern through its MCOs: two or three plans usually cover most managed Medicaid members.

## The three inputs
*1. Payer share.* Above. Tells you which contracts reach patients.
*2. Rate medians.* From the Q1 rate report. Tells you which contracts are worth reaching them with.
*3. Credentialing timelines.* From the November benchmark. Tells you how long each will take.

## Turning three inputs into a rollout
For each state you are considering, list the top payers. For each payer-state pair, note share, median rate for your core codes, and expected credentialing time. Then rank pairs by a simple score: lives reachable per month of credentialing, weighted by rate. The top of that list is where your first applications go. It is rarely the same as an alphabetical or population-based list.

A worked example (illustrative): a behavioral health group choosing between two states finds that State A has higher population but a fragmented commercial market and slow MCO credentialing, while State B has two payers covering 70% of lives, a 90837 median 12% above State A, and a fast Blue plan. State B goes first despite the smaller population. Rate, share, and speed beat headcount.

## What this does to your hiring
Once the payer-state list exists, hire against it. A clinician licensed in the state where your first three contracts land is productive in weeks. One licensed where you have no contracts in progress is idle for a quarter.
