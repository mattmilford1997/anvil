Here is a conversation we have had many times.

"What's your rate with Aetna for a 90837?"
"I don't know. We get $95 a visit from our platform."
"Is that the payer rate or your fee?"
"I think it's our fee."
"What's the payer rate?"
"I'd have to ask."

That founder has insured revenue and no idea what the visit is actually worth.

## How rented economics work
When your clinicians bill under a vendor's contracted entity, the payer pays the vendor's entity the contracted rate. The vendor pays you according to your agreement, typically a fee per visit or a percentage. The difference is the vendor's gross margin. In many arrangements, the contracted rate itself is not disclosed to you, because the contract is between the payer and the vendor's entity, and you are not a party to it.

This is legal and often reasonable at launch. It becomes a problem in three situations.

*When you want to grow margin.* The only lever you have is renegotiating your fee with the vendor. You cannot renegotiate with the payer. You do not know how much room there is.

*When you want to compare.* Is your fee good? You cannot tell without the underlying rate and the market median.

*When you want to leave.* You go to contract your own entity and discover what the payer actually pays. Sometimes it is a pleasant surprise. Sometimes it explains a lot.

## What you should be able to see
1. *The contracted rate per code, per payer, per state,* even during a rented phase. There is no structural reason a platform cannot show you the allowed amount on every claim billed on your behalf.
2. *The platform fee as a separate line.* Not blended into a "per visit" number. If the fee is a percentage of collections, you should see collections.
3. *Benchmarks.* The state and payer medians for your codes, so you know whether the contracted rate is above or below market and where to push at renewal.

## What transparency looks like structurally
A platform that plans for you to own your contracts has no reason to hide the rates; you will see them the day your own contract is live anyway. A platform whose model depends on you staying on its paper has every reason to keep the rate and the fee blended. Which one you are talking to is usually visible in whether they will answer the question "what's the allowed amount on this claim?"

We publish state medians for every code we track, show contracted allowed amounts on every claim during the rented phase, and break the platform fee out as its own line. Not because it is generous, but because the model only works if you can see it.

## Next step

Look up the median for your codes, then compare it to what you're actually receiving.
