Commercial payers are hard to credential with. Medicaid is hard in a different way: every state is its own system, and most states have handed the actual member relationship to three to six managed care organizations that each run their own panel.

## The structure
In most states, a behavioral health group billing Medicaid needs:

1. *State Medicaid enrollment.* Your entity and each clinician enrolled with the state program (provider type, taxonomy, and sometimes site-specific). This is the prerequisite for everything else.
2. *MCO credentialing and contracting.* Each MCO in the state credentials your entity and clinicians and executes its own contract. Panel status varies by MCO and by provider type. A state with five MCOs is five separate processes.
3. *Provider type mapping.* Whether an LPC, LCSW, LMFT, psych NP, or BCBA can enroll, bill independently, or must bill under supervision is a state-by-state rule. Getting this wrong means a denial pattern that takes months to diagnose.
4. *Telehealth allowances.* Which services are payable via telehealth, whether audio-only is allowed, and whether an in-state address is required for the entity or clinician.

## Sequencing for a multi-state BH group
*Enroll with the state program first, always.* MCOs will not process you without it, and some states' FFS enrollment is the slowest step in the whole chain.

*Prioritize MCOs by member share, not alphabetically.* In most states, two MCOs cover the majority of Medicaid BH members. Get those first.

*Map provider types before you hire.* If a state does not allow a given license to bill independently, hiring that license for that state is an idle clinician. Build the hiring plan from the enrollment rules, not the other way around.

*Check telehealth and address rules before you launch.* Some states require an in-state service location or a state-licensed entity. These are formation questions, not credentialing questions, and they need to be answered before the first application goes in.

*Expect variance.* Medicaid MCO timelines in our data run from 60 to 165 days. Plan cash flow accordingly.

## Why this is where generalist vendors struggle
A multi-specialty national platform optimizes for commercial and Medicare Advantage because that is where the standardization is. Medicaid BH is state-specific, provider-type-specific, and low-margin per enrollment. It gets done well by teams that bill it themselves, in those states, every day. That is the operating history behind this guide.
