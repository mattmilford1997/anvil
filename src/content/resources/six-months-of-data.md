We launched this platform with a thesis: care companies should be able to bill insurance nationally in weeks and own their contracts within a year, with full visibility into what they are paid. Six months in, here is what the data says.

## Go-lives
14 companies live across 17 states and 6 specialties. Median time from signed agreement to first paid insured claim: 34 days. Fastest: 19. Slowest: 68, and we explain why below. 71% of customers had their own professional entity formed and a physician owner placed within 30 days of signature. That number should be 100% and it was not; see "what we changed."

## Credentialing
620 enrollments submitted. Median end-to-end: 79 days, against the 97-day median we benchmarked in November. First-submission bounce rate: 9%. The largest driver of variance was still Medicaid MCOs in 3 states; the smallest was the two largest national payers, where automation handled 90% of submissions.

## Rates
The Q1 rate report showed a 34% spread on a 90837 across states. Customers who used the rate data in their rollout order chose different first states than they had planned in 5 of 9 cases. We do not know yet whether that produced better economics; ask us in six more months.

## Eligibility and conversion
Across customers who moved eligibility ahead of the price screen, insured-path booking conversion rose by a median of 2.6x within 30 days. Payer outage incidents detected: 11, across 6 payers. Bookings completed under outage fallback: 214, with 9 resulting in a coverage change at re-check.

## Migration
For the first cohort (customers live 5+ months), 38% of eligible insured volume is now on the customer's own contracts. The first renegotiations are scheduled for July 2027. This is the number the whole model is built on, and we will publish it every quarter.

## What we got wrong
1. *Own-entity formation slipped when launch got busy.* In the first cohort, formation started at signature for only 71% of customers. We changed the onboarding so formation kicks off the same day as roster-add, automatically.
2. *Medicaid variance surprised us in Michigan.* The PIHP carve-out for specialty behavioral health added a second contracting process our benchmark did not model, and it underestimated timelines there by 34 days. Updated.
3. *The rate lookup needed more codes.* Customers asked for 17 codes we did not have on day one, mostly in ABA and integrated care. Added 12 of them.

## What's next
Quarterly publication of go-live, credentialing, rate, conversion, and migration numbers. The Q2 rate report in April. Health plan pages later this year. And every tool on the site stays free and ungated.
