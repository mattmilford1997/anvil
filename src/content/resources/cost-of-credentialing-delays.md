Founders track CAC obsessively and credentialing delays not at all. Both are the cost of getting to revenue. Only one shows up on a dashboard.

## The math
Take a full-time therapist. Assume 100 billable sessions a month at full capacity and a blended in-network rate of $130 for a 90837. That is $13,000 a month in insured revenue. If they are credentialed with your entity but not yet enrolled with the payers that cover your patients, they can bill none of it. They can see cash-pay patients, but you already have clinicians for that demand.

Now a psychiatric nurse practitioner: 120 visits a month, blended $200 across 99214 and 90833 add-ons. $24,000 a month.

Now multiply by your idle roster. Five clinicians hired in a cohort, average 90 days to first billable insured visit: that is somewhere between $180,000 and $360,000 in revenue that did not happen, plus three months of salary paid for capacity you could not sell.

## Why founders miss it
Three reasons. Salary is a visible expense and lost revenue is not. Credentialing lives with ops, not finance, so nobody owns the number. And "it just takes that long" has become an accepted fact in the industry, the way "billing is hard" used to be.

## Four ways to shrink it
*Start enrollment at the offer letter.* The clinician's license, NPI, CAQH, and work history exist before day one. Most companies wait until start date to begin credentialing. That is 30 to 60 days of avoidable idle time.

*Put new hires on an already-contracted roster immediately.* If you are launching or expanding through a platform whose entity already holds payer contracts, roster-adding a clinician takes weeks. Their own entity enrollment can process in parallel. They bill insured visits under the shared entity while their applications are pending.

*Sequence payers by lives, not alphabetically.* Enroll first with the two or three payers that cover most of your patients in that state. A clinician who is in-network with 70% of your demand after four weeks is a productive clinician. One who is in-network with 100% after sixteen weeks cost you three months.

*Track days idle as a KPI.* Put it on the same dashboard as CAC. When it is visible, it gets managed.

## A note for behavioral health
Medicaid MCO enrollment can be slower and more variable than commercial. If Medicaid is a meaningful share of your demand, the idle cost is higher and the sequencing matters more. We cover the state-by-state picture at the end of this month.
